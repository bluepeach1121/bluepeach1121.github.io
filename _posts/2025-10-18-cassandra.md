# Cassandra

For her to see and for all to listen. \
Joyless fate, heard but not believed \
Pristine bearer of words that burn \
And words that would never be false \
“Be gone, gifts that bring no relief. \
Be gone, rituals of agony” \
What is truth that is discarded? \
What is the depth of persuasion? \
All for a burnt future to come \
And for knowledge of raging terror. \
The seer’s body, put to sacrilege \
Unprotected in sanctuary \
Deep injury to wretched omniscience \
Then discarded as the prize of war. \
Relief would not be felt nor accorded \
Neither would pleasant sights be seen \
Only livid welcomes would be given \
By vengeful eyes and a bitter heart. \
At the fated hour, dread appeared \
In fulfilment of final prophecy. \
Prophecy that vowed to take her gifts \
Then ease her from a painful life. \
So she left the living by lethal hands \
Permanent consolation, eternal calm. \
For all to see, for none to yield. \
Cursed insight to an unfair life. 

~Fortune

_[[This poem is inspired by the Trojan priestess named Cassandra. Cassandra was given the gift of prophecy by the god, Apollo. 
But she was cursed so that her prophecy wouldn't be believed. She predicted the fall of Troy, but her voice fell on deaf ears. 
Then during the sack of troy, she was horribly assaulted in the temple of athena and given away as a ‘war gift’ to King Agamemnon. 
On arrival at Mycenea, she was killed by Agamemnon’s wife, Clymenestra, who was vengeful as a result of Agamemnon sacrificing her daughter.]]_
